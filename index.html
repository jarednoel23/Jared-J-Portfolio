Bio

I'm a Communications major at Rollins College with my sights set on law school. Most of what I study comes back to the same question: what actually makes people trust a message, and what makes them tune it out. I've gotten to explore that through research methods, health communication, legal communication, and a community internship at the Orlando Science Center.

Outside of coursework, I'm involved with the Black Student Union, Pre-Law Society, Caribbean Student Union, and Board Game Club at Rollins, and I co-founded the Saint Cloud Pickleball Club back home. I also spend a fair amount of time on a sports commentary account on X, and I play bass, piano, and violin when I get the chance.

03 — Projects

Case Files

COM 285 · Research Methods in Communication

Music, Identity, and Belonging

A mixed-methods research project examining how social, emotional, and identity factors shape college students' music preferences. My team ran semi-structured interviews and built a 20-question Qualtrics survey, then analyzed the results through Uses and Gratifications Theory, Mood Management Theory, Social Identity Theory, and the Five-Factor Model of Musical Preferences.

Methods: Qualitative interviews, quantitative survey design, thematic analysis
Health Communication

Mobile Health: Innovation Meets Ethics

A presentation on how mobile technologies, from telehealth to wearable fitness trackers, are reshaping healthcare access. The project also dug into the ethical tension behind the convenience: data privacy, unequal access to technology, and where healthcare organizations have to draw the line between innovation and responsibility.

Focus: Digital health, data privacy, patient trust
Senior Seminar in Communication

What Actually Makes Someone a Good Listener

An applied look at Active-Empathic Listening, breaking listening down into three measurable stages: sensing, processing, and responding. The most interesting finding was that visible warmth, eye contact, nodding, and smiling barely separated good listeners from bad ones. The real difference showed up in the parts nobody can see you doing.

Focus: Interpersonal communication, professional application
COM 225 · Legal Communication

Communicating Under Legal and Ethical Constraints

Coursework exploring how legal considerations, privacy, defamation, copyright, and free expression, shape how organizations and individuals communicate responsibly. This work sits at the intersection of my communications background and my interest in law, and it's part of what pushed me toward Rollins' Pre-Law Society.

Focus: Legal communication, ethics, credibility
